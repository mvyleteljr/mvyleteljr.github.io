What would change if I fully went after trying to make myself as much money as possible for the rest of this year?

I'm really curious about a few ideas catalyzed by Antifragile. One of which is optionality. I wonder if I genuinely don't want to pursue money and wealth because I don't enjoy it / think it's not worth it, or if I'm just exceptionally bad at it. I want to know the answer. 

I also feel like I want to be 10x more "barbell-y" in my behaviors and way more honest in my approach to doing things. The middle is always the absolute worst, most uninteresting place to be. For instance, what if I truly did just work 4 hours per day in deep work, and then did absolutely anything else interesting to me outside of that? Worked diligently from 10am until about 2pm, then immediately stopped to walk, wander, idle. Only thing to be done outside of that would be brainless admin or calls, if scheduled. Those are simple. 

I think the central problem for me is that life has mostly been *unconscious* as of late, which has made me miserable. I don't want to build more fragile systems to coerce myself like a machine, but I do want to actually make choices. Today, I can start by being done with work immediately after our call today and going to the gym and having a nice long walk afterward with no other goal than to enjoy the walk at the pace I find compelling. 

There are many of these things that I do not do regularly... I wonder what has been missing. 

I'm also interested in gaining "actual self-knowledge" -- I believe there is a fine line between narcissism and building robust self-knowledge that actually serves. I've done both. 

What I've found is that "thinking about oneself" is a complete waste of time, full-stop. 

Thinking is to be deployed towards things where a solution can be obtained, and even then, "thinking" isn't really the right approach. It's tinkering, calculating, playing... but never thinking. To me, most "thinking" is taking bodily sensations and loose experiences in consciousness and trying to rationalize narrative around them. It's a failure. 

Writing isn't thinking, it's writing. And even though some writing can be meta, or circular, like this sentence is, it is still an improvement upon thinking. Often helpful to "put the paddle down".

Point is, I'd like to live an exceptional life full of wonderful experiences, freedom to explore my genuine interests completely, with some robustness or anti-fragility to the natural randomness at play, and independence.  

At present, I don't know how to do that yet, and my primary data source is that I'm still mostly miserable and unfulfilled.

My first hypothesis for what will materially improve my state is to see what happens if I make the genuine pursuit of financial wealth my top priority for a little bit. I'm curious to see what happens. 

Other things I know to be true are that silence, long walks, reducing consumption in favor of production, and taking as many real shots on net are positive. Having skin in the game is positive. Being a good person and taking the opportunity to lean into service for others is also extremely positive. 

We'll pause there for the moment.
