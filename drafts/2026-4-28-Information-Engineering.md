There is a small risk that writing this and posting it publicly diminishes my chance to get any "software engineer" role in the future if things at Concordance go south or I otherwise need to make money outside of my work there. 

And honestly... Thank god. 

I've been battling this feeling in my career for a long time and I think it's time to finally put it to rest. 

*I am not a software engineer.* I have no real interest in software engineering or "writing code" at all. This is not to say I don't enjoy good software that improves my life (Ableton, Obsidian, Ghostty to name a few), but on average, I'm *still* not compelled to "build" in the way that one would expect of a tech/software/coding person at all. 

This reads as a bit odd, as the first stage of my career has been mostly in software based technology, with a brief interlude attempt in music, so what gives?

I'd like to work in consequential fields. I'm excited by being on the frontier of big industries. But also, I *love* information. Pure data. I spend an inordinate amount of time trawling Wikipedia and watching video essays on YouTube. 

This is one of the major improvements (?) to my life post-agent-proliferation. Although I think they exacerbate my OCD in interesting ways that I should write about at some point...

I think all software engineering is in some way "information processing". At the most basic level, we're shuffling bits and representing state in various meaningful ways that can be apprehended at different levels. This is why I've never really been interested in front end engineering. The abstractions are too large for me and I don't have a great sense of the information being shuffled. I'm pulled too far from the critical layer of "raw information".
